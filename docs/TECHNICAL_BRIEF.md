# BRD Arrival Decision Aid — Technical Brief

*AISFS × Merck Animal Health Interdisciplinary Hackathon 2026 · Problem 1: Decision Aid for Predicting BRD in Feedlot Production*

## 1. Decision problem
**User and decision.** The yard operations manager sets the processing protocol for each lot arriving this week. The consulting veterinarian reviews every medication change. The **decision unit is the arrival lot (cohort)**: metaphylaxis and processing are applied per group, and cohort records are what a yard actually holds at arrival. **Decision time is day 0** (arrival and processing), using only information available then. The outcome window is the **first 45 days on feed (DOF)**.

**Options per lot.** (1) Standard care: pull and treat. (2) Arrival metaphylaxis. (3) Enhanced monitoring (twice-daily riding, days 1–14) with low-stress receiving. (4) Both.

**Current operating approach.** The comparator is editable, and labelled as an assumption. By default it is metaphylaxis for lots under 700 lb and standard care otherwise. This default is **generous**: NAHMS 2019 reports that 15.6% of US feedlot cattle were group-treated with injectable antimicrobials. The tool also offers a stricter rule (auction lots under the cut-off only).

**What the tool connects.**

- **Risk:** expected 45-day BRD morbidity per lot, with a 90% range, the main drivers and a confidence label.
- **Action:** a recommended option per lot, with the reason.
- **Consequences:** cases, deaths, antimicrobial regimens and cost, all compared with the current approach.
- **Feasibility:** per-yard rider hours, hospital-pen capacity, an optional antimicrobial cap, and a reported processing-chute check.

## 2. Data and provenance
**No dataset was provided, and we had no permission to use proprietary feedlot records.** All demo data is **synthetic** and freely shareable. It is generated by a seeded script (`src/data/generate.py`) from four yard profiles and a documented data-generating process. Every parameter lives in `config/assumptions.yaml` with a value, a range, a source and a tag:

- **EST**: a published estimate from another population or period.
- **ASM**: a team assumption.
- **OBS** (observed in real data for these yards) is used for nothing, and we say so in the UI.

Arrival variables follow the supplied literature: weight, head count, sex mix, source (ranch-direct, backgrounded or auction), number of commingled sources, transit hours, vaccination history, shrink, pen and bunk space, quarter and weather stress (Rojas et al. 2022; Adekunle et al. 2026a, 2026b). The outcome is the first-treatment BRD case count within 45 DOF. A lot is high risk when morbidity is at least 15% (Rojas et al. 2022).

**Operating differences.** Yard B receives about 530-lb sale-barn calves on long hauls, with tight hospital and rider capacity. Yard A receives about 775-lb ranch-direct yearlings. Historical 45-day morbidity is 6% in Yard A and 18% in Yard B.

**Incomplete information.** Missing values depend on the source. Auction lots lack transit time (26% in the shipped file) and number of sources (35%) far more often than other lots (5–8%). An unobserved lot effect (logit SD 1.3) stands for everything yards do not record.

**Calibration targets.** Mean morbidity is 11%, between Rojas's 6.3% and NAHMS 2011's 21.2%. About 26% of lots are high risk, higher than Rojas's 10.85%; we disclose this. The data dictionary (`data/DATA_DICTIONARY.md`) gives each variable's definition, unit, availability time and generating rule.

**What the data supports:** demonstrating the workflow, how missing records are handled, how constraints bind, and sensitivity analysis. **What it cannot support:** any claim about real-world accuracy, intervention effects, or these yards' performance.

## 3. Methods
**Risk model.** A binomial logistic regression fitted to lot case counts, with missing-value indicators and *metaphylaxis given* as an adjustment covariate. Predictions are made with metaphylaxis set to zero, giving "untreated-equivalent" risk. We chose it because it is explainable for each lot and matched random forest in Rojas 2022 (AUC 0.785 vs 0.789).

- **Uncertainty:** the 90% range is for each lot's *expected* risk (outcomes vary more; P(≥15%) adds the lot-to-lot spread, estimated out of fold). It comes from 100 bootstrap refits with multiple imputation of missing fields from similar historical lots (same yard and source), centred on the average of 10 full-data imputed fits so it does not depend on the random seed. Missing records therefore **widen** the range instead of being hidden.
- **Face validity and drift:** six factors with literature-fixed directions (weight, transit, commingling, shrink, pen and bunk space) are sign-constrained. A drift check compares the last 180 days with a model fitted before them and recalibrates if observed cases differ by more than 2.5 standard errors.
- **Drivers:** each lot's top risk drivers are the grouped linear contributions relative to an average lot.
- **Confidence labels:** a lot is *insufficient data* when at least two of {transit, commingling, shrink} are missing. It is *borderline* when its 90% range crosses 15%.

**Consequence model.** For each lot and option:
- morbidity = untreated risk × (1 − metaphylaxis effect) × (1 − receiving-protocol effect);
- deaths = cases × case fatality × (1 − monitoring effect);
- regimens = metaphylaxis head + cases × (1 + retreatment fraction);
- costs cover treatment, production loss per case, death loss at the feeder price, metaphylaxis, labour and receiving.

**Verdict scope.** *Robust* gates on cost and deaths; regimens and cases are reported beside it, not gated.

**Allocation.** A mixed-integer program (scipy/HiGHS, about 0.3 s, with a greedy fallback) picks one option per lot. It minimises expected cost plus a **stewardship weight** per regimen. That weight is a leadership policy setting, not a market cost, and makes the antimicrobial trade-off explicit.

- **Hard limits:** rider hours per yard, and the **minimum-care floor**:
  - High-risk lots, and borderline lots whose 90% range reaches 15%, never get standard care only.
  - High-risk lots whose whole 90% range is above 15% keep metaphylaxis unless the veterinarian ticks an override. Any step-down is flagged in red for sign-off.
  - Insufficient-data lots never lose the protection they get under the current approach.
- **Soft limits:** hospital-pen peak load and the optional antimicrobial cap carry penalised slack. Any slack is reported as a **capacity shortfall to escalate**, never as quietly rationed care.
- **Binding resources** are reported with the real-dollar value of one more unit.

**Uncertainty in decisions.** 200 paired Monte Carlo draws sample every economic, health and intervention parameter (triangular over its range) together with the risk-model draws. The verdict has three states, with thresholds fixed before results (the corner check was added after stress tests showed a two-state verdict was over-confident):

- **Robust:** the plan is cheaper in at least 70% of draws, has no more deaths in at least 80%, **and** still wins at all 8 corners of the three unvalidated effect ranges.
- **Favourable if assumed effects hold:** the Monte Carlo test passes but some corner loses. The UI shows which corner.
- **Low confidence:** the Monte Carlo test fails. The UI names the criterion.

Tornado charts for cost **and** deaths show which inputs could reverse the advantage.

## 4. Evaluation
All results are on **synthetic data and are circular**: they show the pipeline works, not real-world accuracy. The realistic benchmark is Rojas 2022 on 1,733 real cohorts: AUC 0.68–0.79, PPV 11–25%. Full results are in `docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.md`, reproduced by `python -m scripts.run_validation`.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Temporal hold-out (train before 2026, test 378 lots after) | AUC 0.82, Brier 0.134, calibration slope 1.08, calibration-in-the-large +0.3 pts |
| Baselines, Brier (lower is better) | prevalence 0.182 · weight only 0.158 · yard × source 0.153 · **full model 0.134** |
| Yard never seen (leave one yard out) | AUC 0.71 / 0.74 / 0.83 / 0.78 for yards A–D |
| 90% range covers true expected risk (8 fresh histories) | 90% on average (82–96%); predicted share high-risk 0.37 vs true 0.36 |
| Leakage canaries | scrambling test outcomes leaves predictions unchanged; permuted labels give AUC 0.54 |
| **Decision regret** vs a clairvoyant oracle (60 fresh weeks) | **$5.0k/week** for the plan vs $21.9k for the current rule; truly high-risk lots left on standard care **0** vs 1.37/week |
| Regret when the assumed effects are wrong | extra riding has no effect $11.5k; plus stronger metaphylaxis $13.2k; plus more lot variation and drift $22.8k/week |
| Drift alarm | shipped history z = +1.0 (no alarm); drifting world z = +4.4 (alarm, recalibrated) |

**Key finding: the decision hinges on unmeasured effects, and the verdict says so.** In the base study the simulated truth equals our assumptions, so the regret result shows the model and optimizer work, not that the effects are right. The tool said *robust* in 2 of 60 base weeks (both truly better) and 0 of 25 in each stress world. *Favourable if assumed effects hold* is conditional: where those effects are wrong (S6–S8) the plan cost more in 44–80% of weeks. The corner check shows why. **Deaths rise when extra riding does not lower case fatality and low-stress receiving does little.** **Cost rises when metaphylaxis works better than assumed and the other effects are small.** In our simulations the decision was far more sensitive to these two effect sizes than to the risk-model misspecifications we tested (S1–S3), so the tool's sensitivity points to a pen-level trial of extra riding as the next step.

**Demonstration case** (`docs/DEMO_CASE.md`): 24 lots, 3,406 head, arriving 16–20 Nov 2026, default assumptions.

| Policy | Cases | Deaths | Regimens | Expected BRD cost | Shortfalls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current approach (under 700 lb) | 353 | 24.7 | 3,343 | $166,869 | 2 |
| **Recommended plan** | 363 (+10.6) | 22.0* | **2,504** | **$154,202** | 1 |
| Metaphylaxis for all | 336 | 23.5 | 3,826 | $173,381 | 1 |
| No metaphylaxis | 610 | 42.7 | 763 | $169,090 | 2 (fails care floor) |

The plan removes metaphylaxis from 6 low, moderate or borderline lots (852 head, each flagged for veterinary review). It adds enhanced monitoring to 13 lots and leaves Yard B's hospital pen 0.2 places short (flagged). It is cheaper in 96% of draws, has no more deaths in 94%, and uses fewer regimens in 100%. **Verdict: favourable if assumed effects hold.** It has more deaths at 3 of 8 corners (up to +3.9), each with no extra-riding effect; with metaphylaxis at 65% and no receiving effect, it also costs $6.1k more. \*The death reduction holds only if extra riding cuts case fatality by more than about 4%, an unsourced assumption. Against the stricter auction-only baseline it uses **897 more** regimens but has about 10 fewer deaths. The cost difference is near zero (cheaper in 47% of draws), so the verdict is **low confidence**: the tool reallocates antimicrobials, and whether total use falls depends on current practice.

**Software checks.** Unit, headless and real-browser (Playwright) dashboard tests, plus an adversarial critic suite (`tests/critic/`, log in `docs/CRITIC_LOG.md`) covering determinism, the care floor, coverage, leakage and regret.

## 5. Trade-offs, assumptions and limitations
- **Intervention effects are assumptions.** The metaphylaxis default (45% morbidity reduction, range 25–65%) is consistent with a meta-analysis of randomised trials (Baptiste and Kyvsgaard 2017: pooled RR 0.49) and the 50% Rojas 2022 used; it is not an effect measured in these yards. The monitoring effect on case fatality (20%, range 0–40%) and the receiving-protocol effect (10%, range 0–25%) have **no quantified source**.
- **The death advantage rests on the monitoring assumption.** Below about a 4% reduction in case fatality (the only one-way flip), the plan has more deaths than the current approach. The deaths tornado makes this the first question to settle with the veterinarian.
- **Price years are mixed** (production loss per case $127.58 from 2000–01 data via Rojas, 2011 drug costs, a 2026 feeder-price placeholder), so preventing cases looks cheap relative to deaths. Every price is a manager input.
- **Counting and policy:** a metaphylaxis dose and a treatment course each count as one regimen; hospital load assumes a 21-day onset spread. The stewardship weight is an explicit policy input (the demo plan barely responds to it from $0 to $40).
- **Scope:** allocation is made at day 0. A days 1–14 check-in updates each lot's risk from pulls (binomial update on the arrival prior) and projects hospital load, without re-optimising. A per-head view ranks animals within a lot by synthetic arrival weight (a pen-riding aid, not a diagnosis). Live weather feeds and drug choices are out of scope; no product names or doses, and every medication change needs veterinary review.
- **Using real data:** upload lots and historical records in the same schema to refit; nothing else changes. Replace effects with veterinarian-confirmed or trial-based estimates.
